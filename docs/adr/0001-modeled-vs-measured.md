# 0001 — Synthetic SERP data is presented as modeled, never fused with measured data

## Status

Accepted.

## Context

The lab has exactly one synthetic dataset, `serp_offers` — simulated search
results pages built on *real* `user_id` values sampled from the events feed, but
whose offers, prices, clicks and commissions are generated by a documented model,
not observed. The other three datasets — `orders`, `bookings_legs`, `events`
(and its derived `sessions`) — are real measured feeds.

These layers do not join. Only `user_id` links the clickstream relations
(`events` ↔ `sessions` ↔ `serp_offers`); `orders` and `bookings_legs` carry no
foreign key to that stream, and `orders` does not overlap the clickstream in time.
There is therefore no honest way to present one end-to-end funnel across all of
them.

## Decision

Any ranking or monetisation conclusion drawn from `serp_offers` carries a
**"modeled"** label. Any conclusion drawn from the real islands carries a
**"measured"** label. The two are **never merged** into a single funnel, dataset
or metric.

This follows the project rule that evidence class must not be upgraded: **modeled
is not measured**, and a synthetic number is never reported as an observation.

## Consequences

- Honesty about the evidence class of every number: a reader can always tell
  whether a figure was observed or simulated.
- The dashboard and notebooks keep the two blocks **physically separate** — the
  modeled SERP/ranking block and the measured data block are distinct sections.
- The cost: there is no single end-to-end business funnel. The modeled SERP layer
  cannot be chained into the measured booking/orders feeds, because they share no
  key and no time overlap. That limitation is accepted rather than papered over.
